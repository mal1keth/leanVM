I'd like to help with this on `main`, and want to check scope first.

**History, as far as I can tell from git.** The day before this issue, #523 put a two-scalar BLAKE2s step on `riscv-exploration` (one compression keyed by the state, absorbing up to two scalars, each block tagged with its role and scalar count; a squeeze absorbs the waiting ones in its own step). #552 then replaced it with a framed BLAKE2s duplex and a written argument (DMV18 with a round-by-round adapter), and #607 and #620 followed. `main` kept a BLAKE2s hash chain until #636 replaced it with a duplex over Keccak-f[1600] that tags each absorbed scalar but frames nothing between absorbing and squeezing (after Plonky3's challenger). Nothing in the spec argues that duplex's security.

**`main`'s duplex.** It has one non-injectivity: a permutation forced by a fifth absorbed scalar is the same permutation as a challenge drawn with four scalars waiting. A brute force over all operation sequences up to length 11 finds no other. It could only matter if two transcripts that agree on every permutation input so far (of one proof, of two instances, or of two protocols on the same permutation) could differ in whether the next step squeezes or absorbs. On `main` they can't: how many scalars the verifier absorbs and challenges it squeezes, and in what order, is fixed by the bytecode (hashed into the IV) and the announced sizes (absorbed right after the seed, before any challenge), never by a challenge, another sent value or a Merkle opening, and leanVM's other Fiat-Shamir transcripts start from other seeds. (Keccak-256 also runs on this permutation, and a Merkle leaf's first block can equal the seed's input; that case is the assumption in the Merkle point below.)

How far CO25 goes from there, for the execution proof's transcript (SAFE's theorem needs its IO-pattern tag in the initial capacity, which `main` doesn't have, so CO25's untagged overwrite duplex is the closer fit):

- For a fixed bytecode and fixed sizes, `main`'s transcript is CO25's DSFS (Construction 4.3) with an injective, unbiased codec. Its start is the `h = p` case that CO25 says, informally (§2.2), its analysis covers, with the bytecode entering through its Keccak-256 digest. A literal implementation of Construction 3.3 reproduces `main`'s permutation inputs and challenges on random schedules.
- A prover who chooses the sizes is not covered as stated: in CO25 the protocol's number of rounds is fixed, and the codec fixes their lengths by the security parameter and the instance-size bound (Definition 4.1). CO25's reduction (BackTrack) already recovers a chain's instance before it computes the round offsets it parses with, and the sizes sit in the clear in the first blocks, so I expect those offsets can come from the parsed sizes instead. I haven't written that out. For the deployed guest the bytecode is fixed; for a program the prover also chooses, its length reaches the sponge only through the IV, so the reduction would also need the next point's assumption.
- The Merkle openings bring in CO25's BCS form (§2.6, Corollary 1, stated informally), which adds the Merkle term and keeps the Merkle hash separate from the permutation. The openings fit it (never absorbed, and each root is absorbed before the challenges that pick its queries), but on `main` Keccak-256 runs on the same Keccak-f, so the bound below treats Keccak-256 as a random oracle independent of the permutation, an extra assumption here.

So my current view: the collision is harmless for a reason that can be stated precisely, and the sponge's own term would be about `25t²/2^576`, with the Merkle and IV terms (birthday-scale in 256 bits) dominating the hash loss. But the two gaps above stand between that and a theorem, and the scope is narrower than the issue may want:

- It is about one proof, classical, in the ideal-permutation model. Aggregation needs knowledge soundness (under an honest key a valid signature exists for every message); CO25 carries it over with the same additive term (Theorem 6.2): in rewinding form, or straightline if the protocol's own state-restoration extractor is. Recursion needs it to compose, and the spec doesn't establish the protocol's state-restoration knowledge soundness yet.
- It says nothing about KRS25-style attacks, where the proven computation evaluates the Fiat-Shamir hash. Every leanVM program can evaluate Keccak-f in one `KECCAK` instruction, and the deployed guest does, including to recompute its children's challenges. KRS25's attack also runs GKR, with no committed trace, over a circuit general enough to compute the hash: one the prover picks, or a fixed universal one (their §4). leanVM's uncommitted-trace GKRs are the bus's grand products, which their Remark 3 sets aside, and the Keccak-f circuit, which commits a state every 12 rounds, the kind of depth limit their §5 suggests; the rest of the execution trace is committed. But the model can't rule out an analogue. Nor does it say anything about quantum adversaries.
- It credits no grinding. WHIR's query counts assume 17 bits of it, so without a proof-of-work argument each query phase gives about 111 bits of round-by-round soundness.
- It takes the protocol's state-restoration soundness as given, which the spec establishes only for the opening.

**Cost in the guest** (recursion 2→1, leaves of 900 leanXMSS, log-inv-rate 2): about 920 Fiat-Shamir permutations per child verified, counted in the native verifier (the guest replays each as one `KECCAK`), so roughly 1,840 of the root's ~12,700 `KECCAK` instructions, not counting the root's own batching transcript. Squeezing five challenges per permutation would save about 12% of those, about 2% of the root's `KECCAK`s, so I don't think that's worth a protocol change on its own.
