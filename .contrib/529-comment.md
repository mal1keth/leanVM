I'd like to help with this on `main`, and want to check scope first.

As far as I can tell from git, the day before this issue #523 put a two-scalar BLAKE2s step on `riscv-exploration` (one compression keyed by the state, absorbing up to two scalars, each block tagged with its role and scalar count; a squeeze absorbs the waiting ones in its own step). #552 then replaced it with a framed BLAKE2s duplex and a written argument (DMV18 with a round-by-round adapter), and #607 and #620 followed. `main` kept a BLAKE2s hash chain until #636 replaced it with a duplex over Keccak-f[1600] that tags each absorbed scalar but frames nothing between absorbing and squeezing (after Plonky3's challenger). The spec's §8 describes that duplex and gives no security argument for it. Since Nov 9, `formal/` does, in Lean.

`main`'s duplex has one non-injectivity: a permutation forced by a fifth absorbed scalar is the same permutation as a challenge drawn with four scalars waiting. I brute-forced all operation sequences up to length 11 and found no other. It could only matter if two transcripts that agree on every permutation input so far (of one proof, of two instances, or of two protocols on the same permutation) could differ in whether the next step squeezes or absorbs. On `main` they can't. How many scalars the verifier absorbs and challenges it squeezes, and in what order, is fixed by the bytecode (hashed into the IV) and the announced sizes (absorbed right after the seed, before any challenge). It never depends on a challenge, another sent value or a Merkle opening, and leanVM's other Fiat-Shamir transcripts start from other seeds. I could not find that rule written down, and the Lean does not need it: it decodes each permutation from the verifier program's position (`formal/lean_vm/LeanVM/Protocol/Compile/Rom/Decode.lean`), so it also covers a verifier whose next step depends on what it has read.

`formal/` now settles most of what I had planned to argue. This is my reading of `formal/statement.tex`; I have not built the Lean or read its proofs. `LeanVM.knowledgeSoundness_holds` is straight-line knowledge soundness of a Lean transcription of the verifier in the random-permutation model. The Rust is tied to that transcription by differential tests, not by proof. The adversary outputs the program, the input and the proof, and the error is at most `(Q + 2^14)·2^-128 + 5(Q + 2^14)^2·2^-256` for `Q ≤ 2^128` queries. Keccak-256 is the sponge over the same permutation the duplex calls. The move from two independent oracles to that one permutation is proved in `formal/common/Common/Transfer2.lean`, for a transcript whose direct calls never absorb a block that ends a padded message, and `main`'s duplex qualifies because every call keeps lanes 16..24 (`duplexVm_verifier`). The Lean gets there through a Fiat-Shamir theorem of its own for duplex sponges with proof of work and Merkle commitments (`Compile/Rom/Sound.lean`) and does not use CO25. For one proof and a classical adversary, that is more than I had set out to argue on paper.

Before `formal/` landed I had mapped the execution proof's transcript onto CO25. For a fixed bytecode and fixed sizes it is their DSFS (Construction 4.3) with an injective, unbiased codec, and a literal implementation of Construction 3.3 reproduces `main`'s permutation inputs and challenges on random schedules. Two things kept that from being a theorem. CO25's codec fixes round lengths by the security parameter and the instance-size bound (Definition 4.1), so a prover who chooses the sizes is not covered as stated. And its Merkle form (§2.6, Corollary 1, stated informally) keeps the Merkle hash apart from the permutation. The Lean theorem has neither limit, so I don't think the mapping belongs in the spec. It is in the notes for anyone who wants to read the transcript as an instance of a published construction.

What I think is still open:

- KRS25-style attacks, where the proven computation evaluates the Fiat-Shamir hash. `statement.tex` says its model "excludes, by construction, programs that evaluate their own proof's Fiat-Shamir duplex", and lists soundness across recursion levels as not proved: among other things, the guest verifies its children "with the real Keccak-256 and Keccak-f". Every leanVM program can evaluate Keccak-f in one `KECCAK` instruction, and the deployed guest does, including to recompute its children's challenges. KRS25's attack also runs GKR, with no committed trace, over a circuit general enough to compute the hash: one the prover picks, or a fixed universal one (their §4). leanVM's uncommitted-trace GKRs are the bus's grand products, which their Remark 3 sets aside, and the Keccak-f circuit, which commits a state every 12 rounds, the kind of depth limit their §5 suggests; the rest of the execution trace is committed. That explains why the known attack does not carry over as is. It does not show that no analogue exists.
- Quantum adversaries, which `statement.tex` also lists as not proved. I am not proposing to work on that.

On cost in the guest, I measured `main` at `23a305b`, before the guest rework of #660 and #724, with recursion 2-to-1, leaves of 900 leanXMSS and log-inv-rate 2. Each child verified costs about 920 Fiat-Shamir permutations, counted in the native verifier (the guest replays each as one `KECCAK`). That is roughly 1,840 of the root's ~12,700 `KECCAK` instructions, not counting the root's own batching transcript. Squeezing five challenges per permutation would save about 12% of those, about 2% of the root's `KECCAK`s. I don't think that is worth a protocol change on its own, even less now that `formal/` would have to follow it.

Questions:

1. Is `main` the target for this issue now, or is `riscv-exploration`'s transcript the one that matters? And for `main`, does `formal/` close it?
2. Which construction, and what about it, did the concern refer to: #523's step, `main`'s BLAKE2s chain, or duplex challengers in general? `main` is now the last kind, so I'd like whatever I write to answer what was actually seen.
3. If something is still wanted here, which would be useful: a sentence in the spec's §8 on the non-injectivity, with a pointer to how `formal/` treats it; or a written analysis of how far KRS25's attack is from leanVM's GKRs and its recursion step? I'd rather do the one you want than guess.
